Glossary

 Term

 Meaning
Aumbry A recess in a wall in which to store liturgical vessels
Blind arcade A series of arches in a wall that have no windows or doors, the interstices being filled with brick of stone
Broach spire A spire (usually octagonal) rising directly out of a tower
Chancel The area near the altar of a church that is primarily reserved for clergy and choir
Chantry A process by which the church was endowed to pray for the soul or souls of a person or family.  Typically this would involve payments to a priest for saying masses, and a special chapel might be created for these services.
Decorated style A style of architecture that developed in between c1250 and c1350, characterised by elaborate window tracery with stone mullions (verticals) topped by ogee (S-curved) arches and floral motives
Gothic style The style of architecture that predominated in England from c1180 to c1520.  It is characterised by pointed arches, vaulted roofs, buttresses, spires and large windows.

It is usually divided into Early Gothic, with simply pointed arches, Decorated (see above) and Perpendicular (see below)
Lancet window A long, narrow window with a pointed arch
Mullion A vertical window divider
Nave The part of the church that is reserved for the congregation
Norman The style of architecture that preceded Gothic.   It was used in the early years after the Norman Conquest and is characterised by round arches and  massive walls and columns
Perpendicular style This was the last style of Gothic architecture and predominated between c1350 an c1520.  It is characterised by very large windows and pillars that consist of a cluster of 'rods' that rise without a break from floor to roof and then either splay out into an array of interlocking branches or support hammerbeam roofs.
Pilaster A column that projected slightly from a wall and which is typically rectangular in section
Piscina A basin for washing hands and liturgical articles
Rendering A covering of the exterior of the walls of a building, usually consisting of a mixture of sand, cement and stone
Reredos A screen behind the altar of a church, usually decorated with religious images
Saxon style This term covers a variety of architectural types the were used before the Norman Conquest.  Those that have survived are characterised by brick or stone buildings with blind arcading, pilasters  and triangular headings for windows and doors.
Sedilia Stone seats let into the chancel wall for the use of clergy
Squint A hole that enables some of the worshippers in side chapels to see the priest at the main altar (see Godalming Parish Church's Lady Chapel and Hascombe Church's Lady Chapel)
Stucco A plaster covering of a wall, finer than rendering and often used as a decorative medium.
Transept An extension to a building that lies a right angles to its main line
Transom A horizontal window divider
